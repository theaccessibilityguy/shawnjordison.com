Shawn Jordison · My story

The story behind the work.

I got into accessibility as a student worker at Columbia College, a small community college in Northern California. I was studying computer science, so they placed me in the disability services department. That’s where I fell in love with figuring out software and using it to help students.

I taught myself how to emboss braille, create audio files, and produce large print. I didn’t have tutorial videos to walk me through it. I learned by doing, and eventually helped digitize the department and its forms.

Disability was already part of my life. I have Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease, or CMT, a neurological condition that affects the peripheral nerves involved in movement and sensation. Growing up, I worried about my body failing and whether I’d be able to keep working. That was part of what pushed me toward office technology work.

At Santa Monica College, I became a tenured faculty member teaching students with disabilities how to use their assistive technology to succeed. I also helped change how the department worked, building accessible forms and digital systems adopted by 50 staff members. We could find information faster, help students more quickly, and spend less time on endless data entry.

From there, I joined the California Community Colleges Accessibility Center, supporting all 116 colleges with complex accommodations and training. Alongside that work, I reviewed online courses with CVC-OEI and helped develop an accessibility rubric used by thousands.

Those experiences led me to start The Accessibility Guy. Today, we help organizations big and small make their content and processes more accessible. The work brings together technical expertise, teaching, and helping people manage change.

I also share what I’ve learned through tutorials, workshops, speaking, and The Accessibility Guy Podcast. Figuring out software is still a big part of what I do. So is helping other people understand it well enough to use it in their own work.
